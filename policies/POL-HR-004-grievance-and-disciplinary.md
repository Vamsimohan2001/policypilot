# Grievance and Disciplinary Policy

**Purpose:** This restricted policy defines the formal process for raising and resolving workplace grievances and the framework for fair, proportionate disciplinary action. It is restricted to HR because it contains investigation procedures and confidential case-handling mechanics.

## Raising a Grievance

A grievance may be raised formally by submitting a written complaint to the HR portal or to any HR business partner. The complaint should describe the issue, dates, individuals involved, and any supporting evidence. Employees may be accompanied by a colleague at any meeting. HR acknowledges every formal grievance within 5 business days.

## Grievance Stages

Stage 1: the HR business partner mediates a resolution with the parties. Stage 2: if unresolved, a formal investigation is opened, typically completed within 20 business days, with interviews and document review conducted by a trained investigator. Stage 3: the Grievance Committee, composed of a senior HR leader and a neutral business leader, hears the case and issues a written outcome within 10 business days of the investigation report. Outcomes may include mediation agreements, process changes, or referral to the disciplinary process.

## Appeals

Either party may appeal a Stage 3 outcome within 10 business days by writing to the Chief People Officer, stating the grounds of appeal. The CPO reviews the record, may re-interview key witnesses, and issues a final, binding decision within 15 business days. Appeals are limited to one per case.

## Disciplinary Framework

The disciplinary process applies to misconduct and policy breaches and follows progressive stages: (1) documented verbal warning, (2) written warning valid for 6 months, (3) final written warning valid for 12 months, and (4) termination. Serious misconduct, including harassment, violence, fraud, theft, deliberate security breaches, or falsification of records, may result in immediate termination without prior warnings.

## Investigations and Fairness

Every disciplinary investigation must give the employee advance written notice of the allegations, the opportunity to respond in writing, and the right to be accompanied. Timelines: investigation 10 business days for standard cases, 20 for complex cases; the employee receives the outcome in writing within 5 business days of the decision. Suspension during investigation is with full pay and is precautionary, not a sanction.

## Record Keeping and Confidentiality

Case records are stored in a restricted HR system accessible only to Employee Relations staff. Records are retained for 7 years or as local law requires, whichever is longer. All participants must maintain confidentiality of the proceedings. Breach of confidentiality by handling staff is itself a disciplinary matter.
