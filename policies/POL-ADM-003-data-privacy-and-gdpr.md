# Data Privacy and GDPR Compliance Policy

**Purpose:** This policy defines how the company handles personal data of employees, customers, and partners in line with the GDPR and equivalent privacy laws: lawful bases, subject rights, retention, transfers, breach notification, and accountability.

## Lawful Bases and Consent

Personal data may be processed only under a documented lawful basis: contract, legal obligation, legitimate interest, vital interest, public task, or consent. Consent must be freely given, specific, and revocable, and is used only where no other basis applies. Processing activities are recorded in the Record of Processing Activities (RoPA), maintained by the DPO and reviewed quarterly.

## Data Minimisation and Purpose Limitation

Only data necessary for the documented purpose may be collected. Fields are reviewed at system design and annually thereafter; unused fields are deleted. Purpose changes require a privacy review before new processing begins. Special-category data (health, biometrics, union membership, beliefs) requires an Article 9 condition and DPO sign-off.

## Data Subject Rights

Individuals may exercise rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and objection by contacting the privacy mailbox or the DPO. The company must acknowledge a request within 5 business days and complete it within 30 calendar days, extendable once by 60 days for complex requests with written notice. Identity is verified before disclosure, using proportionale measures that do not demand excessive data.

## Retention and Deletion

Personal data is retained only as long as the retention schedule requires: recruitment records 12 months after a hiring decision; employee records 7 years after separation where tax law requires; financial transaction records 7 years; marketing consent records until withdrawal plus 24 months. Deletion runs are executed quarterly from the central schedule, and systems must support verified erasure of backups through key destruction or equivalent.

## International Transfers

Transfers outside the EEA/UK require an adequacy decision, Standard Contractual Clauses, or another Chapter V mechanism. Transfer impact assessments are completed for all new vendors before onboarding. The DPO maintains the transfer register and reviews it semiannually.

## Vendors and Processors

Every processor must sign a Data Processing Agreement covering security, sub-processors, breach notification, and audit rights. Vendor privacy reviews are required at onboarding and annually for high-risk vendors. A vendor list with data categories and locations is maintained by Procurement and the DPO.

## Breach Notification

A personal-data breach must be reported to the DPO within 24 hours of discovery through the incident process. Where the breach is likely to result in risk to individuals, the supervisory authority must be notified no later than 72 hours after the company becomes aware. Affected individuals are informed without undue delay where there is a high risk. All breaches are logged in the breach register, including near-misses.

## Accountability

The DPO reports quarterly to the executive team on privacy risks, requests, and breaches. Privacy-by-design reviews are mandatory for projects processing personal data. Staff receive privacy training annually, and HR processes follow the Employee Data Privacy Notice.
