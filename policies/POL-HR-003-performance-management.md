# Performance Management Policy

**Purpose:** This policy explains how performance is set, reviewed, rated, and improved: goal setting, review cycles, the rating scale, calibration, feedback, promotions, and performance improvement plans.

## Goal Setting

Every employee must have documented goals in the performance system within 30 days of joining or of the start of a review cycle. Goals should number between 4 and 7, with at least one development goal. Managers and employees review goal progress in monthly one-on-ones and adjust goals formally at the mid-cycle checkpoint.

## Review Cycle

The company runs two formal review cycles per year: April (covering the previous October-March period) and October (covering April-September). Each cycle includes a self-review, manager assessment, and peer feedback from at least 3 colleagues. All feedback must be submitted in the performance system by the published deadline; late submissions delay calibration for the whole team.

## Rating Scale and Calibration

Performance is rated on a 5-point scale: 1 (needs improvement), 2 (partially meets), 3 (meets expectations), 4 (exceeds), and 5 (outstanding). Ratings are calibrated in cross-team sessions chaired by the function head to ensure consistency. Distribution guidance is advisory, not forced: approximately 60-70 percent of ratings should be 3, with 4 and 5 reserved for clearly exceptional impact. Ratings are shared with the employee in a dedicated review conversation within 10 business days of calibration.

## Feedback and Continuous Improvement

Managers must hold monthly one-on-ones focused on progress, blockers, and development. Feedback should be specific, timely, and behavioural. The company encourages upward feedback and a 360-degree pulse survey twice a year; results are aggregated and never used punitively against individual respondents.

## Promotions

Promotion windows coincide with the two review cycles. Promotion requires sustained performance at the next level for at least two consecutive cycles, a written business case from the manager, and approval by the function head and HR. Promotion is based on demonstrated scope and impact, not tenure. Salary changes following promotion are governed by the Compensation and Benefits Policy.

## Performance Improvement Plans

When performance falls below expectations, the manager must first hold a documented feedback conversation. If no improvement occurs within 30 days, HR is engaged and a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) may be issued. A PIP runs for 60 days, with weekly checkpoints and objective success criteria. PIPs are not automatic precursors to termination; employees who meet the criteria exit the PIP successfully. Where a PIP is not met, the process transitions to the disciplinary framework with HR guidance.

## Record Keeping

All formal review documents, ratings, and PIP records are stored in the HR system and are confidential. Records are retained as required by local employment law and accessible only to HR, the employee, and the management chain as needed for legitimate administration.
